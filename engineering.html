01 — Case study

Automated Material Mover

Co-designed and prototyped an autonomous forklift system for industrial material transport under strict hardware constraints — integrating mechanical design, electronics, sensing, and iterative reliability testing.

01

Project objective

Design an autonomous material mover (AIBM4) that transports materials within a factory environment — removing manual handling, improving productivity, and supporting a safer, more sustainable workplace.

02

Design & mechanism

An iterative design process converged on a three-wheeled automated forklift with differential steering around a central axle, chosen for maximum manoeuvrability within factory aisles.

03

Sensing & autonomy

Tape-sensing technology enables precise, repeatable movement within the factory environment, giving the autonomous workflow a reliable basis for navigation without manual forklift operation.

04

Mechanical design

Steel body and forks for strength and sustainability, with airless tyres chosen to improve durability and reduce waste over the system's lifetime.

05

Validation & standards

Engineering calculations validate the design's structural integrity and feasibility, with the design targeting compliance with ISO 3691-4 for driverless industrial trucks.

06

Cost & viability

An in-depth cost analysis demonstrates that automated guided vehicles are financially viable against conventional manual forklifts, driven by savings in labour and operating costs over time.

07

Risk & reliability

A structured risk assessment mitigates operational hazards, supported by an iterative design process and repeated reliability testing of the prototype cycle.

08

Outcome

A validated feasibility study and design blueprint for an autonomous material mover, documented in a 15-page report co-authored by Group 19-AIBM4 at Durham University (July 2026).
